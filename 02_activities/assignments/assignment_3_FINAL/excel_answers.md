Excel Visualization 2: Auto Theft Rates Across Toronto Neighbourhoods (2025): Highest and Lowest 10

# Software Used

This visualization was created in Microsoft Excel. Excel was used to sort and filter neighbourhoods by their 2025 auto theft rate and generate a horizontal bar chart.

# Intended Audience

The intended audience includes community members, policymakers, and researchers interested in spatial differences in crime rates. The format is accessible to a general audience.

# Message

This visualization highlights the 10 neighbourhoods with the highest and the 10 with the lowest auto theft rates in 2025. The main message is that there is substantial variation across Toronto neighbourhoods. Presenting both extremes in a single chart emphasizes the scale of inequality.

# Design Considerations

A horizontal bar chart was selected because neighbourhood names are long and easier to read horizontally. The neighbourhoods were ordered by rate to make comparisons intuitive. Unnecessary elements such as extra legends were removed to reduce clutter. Data labels were included to improve clarity, and the axis was clearly labeled with units (per 100,000 residents). The title was written in a neutral, descriptive way.

# Reproducibility

Excel is less reproducible than Python because filtering and formatting steps are done manually. However, the dataset is publicly available and the selection criteria (highest and lowest 10 neighbourhoods in 2025) are clearly defined. Anyone using the same dataset and criteria could replicate the result, even if formatting differs slightly.

# Accessibility

Accessibility was considered by using a horizontal layout, readable font sizes, and clear labeling. The visualization does not rely solely on color to communicate differences. The simple layout reduces cognitive load.

# Impacted Communities

Neighbourhoods identified as having high rates may experience reputational effects or stigma. The visualization is intended to inform rather than assign blame. Community organizations and policymakers may use this information to guide safety strategies.

# Feature Selection

The dataset includes many crime types and years. I focused on auto theft in 2025 to maintain clarity and avoid overcrowding. Rates were used instead of raw counts to ensure fair comparison across neighbourhoods with different population sizes.

# Underwater Labour

The background work included sorting and filtering neighbourhoods, verifying rate values, testing separate versus combined charts, adjusting formatting for readability, correcting labeling errors, and refining the layout to clearly show contrast between high and low rates.
